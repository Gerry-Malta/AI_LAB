# Camera movement

**Move when something motivates it.** A motivated move answers the scene: a character moves, attention shifts, a
reveal, an emotional transition. Unmotivated moves are a style choice that loses its force when overused; a camera
should never drift out of boredom.

| move | motivated by | effect |
|---|---|---|
| push-in (dolly in) | a decision, a realisation, a line that lands | intimacy, emphasis; slow = subtle, fast = shock |
| pull-out | a reveal of context, a character left alone | isolation, consequence, release |
| follow (Steadicam, dolly with) | a character's movement | we travel with them; whose scene it is |
| pan / tilt | a look, a gesture, an entrance | attention moves like the viewer's eyes |
| reveal (start tight or occluded, open) | new information | surprise, discovery |
| crane / drone descent | entering a place | from the map to the people |
| static | the performance carries it | weight, patience, observation |

Speed and timing:
- Slow and smooth moves read as natural and emotional; fast ones as abrupt and stylised.
- Start the move on its motivation (an actor gets up, a line lands) and let it **settle before the cut**:
  `move: [start_s, end_s]` in Layout Studio keeps the camera still outside the window.
- The rig sets real limits (dolly track, crane reach, walking speed); the shot check enforces them.
- Restraint: if every shot moves, emotional shifts lose their contrast. Keep the move for the moment that needs it.

Editing implication: a cut from a moving shot into a static one is abrupt unless the movement is matched or the move
has settled. Cutting "in movement" (on a character's action) hides the cut — see the film-editor skill.
