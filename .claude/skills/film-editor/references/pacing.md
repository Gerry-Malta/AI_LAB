# Pacing and rhythm

## Numbers to know
- **Average shot length (ASL) has fallen for decades.** Reporting on James Cutting's work: from about 8–10 s in the
  1960s to about 3–4 s by the mid-2000s (other counts give ~12 s in 1930 to ~2.5 s today). Bordwell's intensified
  continuity describes the same trend with closer framings and freer cameras. The decline started before MTV and
  kept its pace after.
- **Variation is a structure, not noise.** Cutting, DeLong & Nothelfer (Psychological Science, 2010) found that the
  sequence of shot lengths in many films, especially after 1980, approaches a 1/f pattern — the same fluctuation
  structure as human attention — and that this pattern is independent of the mean. Shot length should vary with the
  content, at several scales.
- **Structure shapes length.** Cutting, Brunick & DeLong (2011): acts shape shot lengths and transitions across 150
  Hollywood-style films; Cutting (2016): shot durations pace the narration — longer in calmer sections, shorter in
  action — together with motion, luminance and music.
- **Shot scale is a style signature.** Barry Salt's statistical style analysis treats ASL and the distribution of
  shot scales as defining features of a director's style (Cinemetrics).

## Targets used by the engine (ASL, seconds)
contemplative 6–12 · drama 4–7 · dialogue 3–6 · tension 2–4 · action 1.2–3. They are starting points for a
conversation with the material, not quotas.

## Reading the edit report
- **CV** (standard deviation / mean of the durations): below ~0.35 the cut reads mechanical; dramatic scenes often
  sit around 0.5–0.8. Four or more consecutive shots within ±15 % of each other are flagged as a monotone stretch.
- **Acceleration** (mean of the first third / mean of the last third): above 1 means the cutting quickens toward the
  end; build toward the peak, then release.
- **Need vs duration:** each shot needs time for its lines (+ a beat), its move, its action and to be read the first
  time (a new place or face needs longer). The engine's reading times are working values: EWS 3.0 s, WS 2.4, FS 2.0,
  MLS 1.8, MS 1.6, MCU 1.3, CU 1.1, BCU 0.9, ECU 0.8, insert 1.2, +1 s the first time a place is seen.
- **Holds:** more than ~3.5 s after the last event is flagged — keep it if the silence is the emotion.

## Eisenstein's methods of montage (a vocabulary for rhythm)
- **Metric:** lengths by formula, like bars of music; tension by shortening while keeping proportions.
- **Rhythmic:** lengths follow the movement inside the frame; continuity of motion.
- **Tonal:** cutting by the dominant emotional tone of the shots.
- **Overtonal:** the combined effect of metric, rhythmic and tonal relations.
- **Intellectual:** meaning from the collision of images.
A narrative scene is mostly rhythmic and tonal; a metric passage (a countdown, an assault) is a deliberate effect.
